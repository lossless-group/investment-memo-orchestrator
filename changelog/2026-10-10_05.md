## Why care?

This is plan 2 of the connector spec (`context-v/specs/MemoPop-Connector-API.md`),
the engineering half: everything that can be built and proven on a laptop
before anyone touches Railway. After it, deploying `memopop.didi.sh` is a list
of settings, not a project, and a broken or slow step in production shows up
within half an hour as a failed GitHub Actions run that says which step.

## What landed

**The image** (`Dockerfile`, `.dockerignore`). Python 3.13 slim with uv, the
lockfile's dependencies, pandoc, WeasyPrint's pango, cairo, and gdk-pixbuf
libraries with fonts, poppler-utils, tesseract, ghostscript, ImageMagick, and
jj 0.46.0 for the build's architecture. Only `src/`, `cli/`, `templates/`, and
three scripts go in; `.dockerignore` is an allowlist, so `io/`, `.env`, the
root's logs, and outputs never enter the build context. The entrypoint makes
`MEMO_IO_ROOT` on the volume, gives it to an unprivileged `memopop` user, and
runs uvicorn on `$PORT` with `--proxy-headers --forwarded-allow-ips='*'`.
Built for linux/amd64: about 400 MB compressed, 1.3 GB unpacked.

**What it serves** (`src/connector/serve.py`): the connector alone, plus
`/healthz` (503 when the workspace root isn't writable; says whether jj is
present). It does not serve `src.server.app`: that app's Tauri sidecar routes
have no authentication and read and write firm data, which is fine on a
laptop's loopback and not on a public host.

**Railway** (`railway.json`): Dockerfile builder, one replica, health check
`/healthz`, restart on failure.

**The health check** (`scripts/health_check.py`, CONN-LIVE-01). It creates a
uniquely named deal in `test-firm`, adds an inline note, follows `next_step`
until the deal is done (one approved research step, one draft, canned
enhancements, a skip where canned text can't meet a step's checks), compiles,
and downloads the signed HTML and PDF. Every call is timed against its
documented worst case plus a network allowance. It exits 1 with
`FAIL <step>: <reason>` on any failure or slow call, and 2 without a key. It
needs only httpx. It runs on a one-section `health-check` outline installed in
test-firm's own templates, so no other firm sees it.

**The schedule** (`.github/workflows/live-health.yml`): every 30 minutes and
on dispatch, the health check and CONN-LIVE-02 against
`https://memopop.didi.sh`, using the repository secret `MEMOPOP_HEALTH_KEY`.
Until that secret exists, each run skips with a notice instead of failing.

**Provisioning** (`src/connector/provision.py`, `scripts/provision_firm.py`):
creates or updates a firm without replacing its `firm.json`, records its
didi.sh entity id, optionally installs the health-check outline, and prints a
fresh static key without storing it.

**Per-firm buckets** (`src/connector/config.py`, `bucket.py`). Writing the
deploy guide showed that Railway generates each bucket's name and gives each
its own keys, so one global key pair and a `memopop-{firm}` template can't
reach a second firm's bucket. `MEMOPOP_S3_FIRM_<FIRM>_<FIELD>` now sets one
firm's bucket, keys, endpoint, and region, falling back to the globals.
Addressing is virtual-hosted by default, as Railway's buckets require.

**Materials that never finish** (a plan 4 follow-up, in
`src/connector/materials/pipeline.py`). An upload link that expired unused,
or a fetch a restart dropped, used to leave a material `queued` forever, so
`materials_pending` never reached zero. Such materials now become
`material_unreadable` skips with a reason: on startup (every job in flight is
lost), and when `list_deals` or `next_step` reads a deal (an upload five
minutes past expiry, any other job thirty minutes past its start). A job that
finishes after the sweep leaves the skip alone. Tested in
`tests/connector/test_materials_sweep.py` (unmarked; the spec already promises
materials never block).

**The operator guide** (`docs/operator/deploy.md`): every variable with its
production value and whether it's a secret, the volume, a bucket per firm,
provisioning a firm with its entity mapping and static key, switching a step
off, reading the health check, pruning health-check deals, and the first
deploy in order.

## Proven

- CONN-LIVE-01 GREEN: the script against a real uvicorn passes; a missing
  outline, a bad key, an unreachable server, a slow `next_step`, a compile
  answering `not_implemented`, and a broken compiled link each exit 1 naming
  the step.
- The image built locally for linux/amd64 (about 5 minutes cold under
  emulation on Apple silicon) and, run with a local volume, passed the health
  check end to end, real compile and PDF included, before and after a
  container restart. That run caught one real bug: compile imports
  `cli/export_branded.py`, which the first image left out.

## Not tested

- **CONN-LIVE-02** is GATED: it needs the deployed service. The VP Eng runs it
  after the first deploy (`MEMOPOP_LIVE_URL=https://memopop.didi.sh`).
- **Railway itself**: the volume's ownership as Railway mounts it, `railway ssh`
  as the provisioning route, `/healthz` as Railway's deploy gate, and proxy
  headers through Railway's edge were exercised only in local Docker.
- **Real S3**: per-firm bucket settings are checked by building boto3 clients,
  not by writing to a Railway bucket. Whether presigned compile links work
  with Railway's endpoint and `auto` region is untested.
- **The workflow** has not run on GitHub: its sparse checkout and skip-without-
  secret paths were reproduced locally, not in Actions.
- **Budgets under real network**: the 2-second allowance is a guess until the
  check has run against production for a few days.
- The startup sweep assumes one server process per volume; two would sweep
  each other's in-flight jobs.
