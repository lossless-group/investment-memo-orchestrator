```
fix(connector): Give the same bytes at several paths in a deal one material

The uploader keeps one material per distinct sha256 in a deal, listing every
path it was found at in local_paths. The manifest stays keyed by path, and each
of those entries carries the material's id. check_upload.py matches through
local_paths and flags a manifest entry that names another material.

Also included: CONN-UPL-04 in the spec and plan, the issue resolved, plan step 0
ticked.
```

## Why Care?

A binary's `material_id` comes from its bytes. A deck saved in `inputs/` and again in a run's `images/` folder gave the deal two material records with one id. Anything that looks a material up by id (`get_artifact` on `material:<id>`, a revision's `material_ids`, `gather_inputs()`) found an ambiguous record. Unreadable copies also made one skip per path. This had to be fixed before the first `--apply`, or the server's `deal.json` files would carry the duplicates.

## What's New?

- **`scripts/upload_firm.py`.** The first path with given bytes, in sorted order, makes the material. Later paths with the same bytes are added to its `local_paths`, with no second record, text file or skip. `local_path` stays on the record as the first path.
- **The manifest** (`binaries-on-server.yaml`) still has one entry per path. Each entry carries the shared `material_id`, including entries carried over from an earlier manifest.
- **`scripts/check_upload.py`** reads `local_paths` (falling back to `local_path`) to match manifest entries, and reports `material_id_differs` when a manifest entry names a material other than the server's.
- **`scripts/apply_upload.py`** doesn't read material records, so it needed no change.
- **Tests.** Two CONN-UPL-04 tests. Two earlier assertions that expected one material per path (the compiled-v1 test, and CONN-UPL-01's) now expect one material carrying three paths. That change was made in the red commit, since those assertions encoded the duplicate ids.

## What Isn't Tested

- **Deals already on the server.** Their `deal.json` files come from the October import, which recorded no binaries, so there is nothing there to dedupe. A deal staged by the previous uploader and applied would still hold duplicates until it's uploaded again. None has been applied.
- **Nothing ran against the server.** As before, the uploader, apply and the check are tested on local folders only.
