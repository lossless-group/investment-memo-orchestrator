```
feat(citations): Repair memos cited across sections, as hex keys

Memos assembled before the citation-collision fix link most footnotes to
another section's source. cli/repair_citations.py (logic in
src/citation_repair.py) maps each section's markers back to its own research
and rewrites them with hex keys from src/citation_keys.py (Part 2's module,
copied byte for byte so the branches merge cleanly; assign_keys() runs once
over the whole memo's definitions; no deck or dataroom prefixes). Dry run by default; --write keeps a .bak; --out repairs a
copy; --export re-exports dark and light HTML and PDF; --scan lists affected
memos and flags duplicated deals, collapsed research keys and version dirs with
several finished memos.

Sections it can't prove (collapsed research keys, edited after assembly,
heading missing or repeated) stay byte-identical with their numbered keys.
Text with no research file keeps its current source, in hex, reported
unverified. The prose guard refuses to write if anything but markers and the
block changed.

Also included: the repair-memo-citations skill; manage-memo-citations now
points at hex keys instead of semantic names; the pipeline reference lists the
new CLI.
```

## Why

Each section's research numbers its sources from 1, and the old assembler treated those numbers as global, so a section's `[^25]` linked to whichever section defined `[^25]` first. New runs assemble correctly, but finished memos and their exports stay wrong until repaired, and a scan found about 40 affected versions. Writing the repaired keys as hex keys derived from the source means a repaired memo can't collide again, and moves MemoPop onto the rule Lossless Flavored Markdown already set.

## What was tested, and what wasn't

- 15 tests in `tests/test_repair_citations.py`, plus Part 2's `tests/test_citation_keys.py` (6) carried with its module, all on small synthetic memos: a collided memo repaired to hex with prose unchanged; a collapsed-key section and an edited section refused byte-identical; Executive Summary and Closing kept on their current source and reported unverified; nothing repairable leaves the memo untouched; two finished memos require `--memo`; dry run writes nothing; `--write` keeps a `.bak`; the prose guard refuses to write; `source_key()` stability, normalisation, plain keys for deck and dataroom sources, and widening both keys to eight characters on a forced collision; the scan's wrong-link count and duplicate flag.
- Before committing, the tool ran with `--out` into a scratch directory on copies of two real finished versions. On the first it changed 62 links, every one of 299 markers now points at the same source as the earlier hand-proven repair, and prose was unchanged; three sections were refused as edited after assembly. The second was refused whole for collapsed research keys. Nothing under `io/` was written.
- **Not tested:** `--export` (it calls `cli/export_branded.py` for each mode; the brand configs live in the private firm repos, which this worktree doesn't have). A quick Pandoc conversion confirmed hex footnotes render numbered 1, 2 in order. `--scan` has not run across the real `io/` tree with this code; the earlier scratch scan used the same logic. `--write` hasn't run on a firm's memo.

## What's next

The operator runs `--scan io`, then `--write --export` on the newest finished version of each affected deal, commits each firm's changes in its own repo with a changelog note, and tells partners which sections were refused. Part 2 of the plan (`connector/hex-citations`) makes new runs and the connector draft in hex keys from the same `source_key()`.
