## Why care?

Until now the server only moved forward. Once a section was drafted, nothing
could reopen it, so a colleague holding the latest PDF had no way to say *"we
have new market research, can it go in?"* and have Claude act on it. The
memos were shareable but not iterable, which was the whole point of putting
them on the server.

## What changed

- **`start_revision`** reopens one or several sections, with the colleague's
  angle in their own words and any new materials. It starts from `research`
  (new evidence) or `draft` (same evidence, new framing).
- **`next_step` walks only what the revision reopened:** the sections'
  research, drafts, and section enhancements, then the summaries and
  one-pager. Each step comes with its previous submission and the new
  materials as inputs, and an instruction that leads with the request.
- **The colleague's own approval** of revised research is enough.
- **`compile` closes the revision** and labels the memo with the next patch
  version (an imported `v0.2.2` compiles as `v0.2.3`). `list_deals` shows each
  deal's `memo_version` and any open revision.
- **`cancel_revision`** puts every changed section back exactly as it was,
  from a copy taken when the revision opened, at a new version.
- **One open revision per deal**, covering any number of sections.

## Found on the way

- **The current text of a section is now the most recently saved one.** It
  used to be the last in step order, so a revised draft would have lost to an
  older citations pass of the old draft.
- **Diagrams render to the same bytes every time.** The SVG embedded a random
  id and a date, so every recompile of a memo with a diagram looked like a
  change and bumped the version.
- **A new material no longer interrupts a revision with a materials brief.**

## What's next

- Deploy, then have a colleague at an imported firm revise a section from Claude Desktop.
- A revision that skips the summaries keeps the old ones; the compile report
  lists the skip, but a later pass could flag summaries that predate a revised
  section.
